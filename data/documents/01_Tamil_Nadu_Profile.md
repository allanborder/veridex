# Tamil Nadu — State Profile

## Section 1: Overview and Identity

Tamil Nadu is a state located in the southernmost part of the Indian subcontinent. It is the eleventh largest state in India by area, covering approximately 130,058 square kilometres. Tamil Nadu is bordered by the states of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana to the north, Karnataka to the northwest, and Kerala to the west. To the south and east, Tamil Nadu is bounded by the Indian Ocean, the Palk Strait, and the Bay of Bengal.

The official language of Tamil Nadu is Tamil, one of the world's oldest classical languages with a literary tradition spanning over 2,000 years. Tamil is spoken by approximately 80 million people worldwide and holds the status of a classical language recognised by the Government of India.

## Section 2: Capital and Major Cities

The capital of Tamil Nadu is Chennai, formerly known as Madras. Chennai is also the largest city in the state and serves as a major commercial, cultural, and educational hub in South India. Chennai is the fourth largest city in India by population.

Other major cities in Tamil Nadu include Coimbatore, Madurai, Tiruchirappalli (also known as Trichy), Salem, Tirunelveli, Vellore, Erode, and Thoothukudi (Tuticorin). These cities serve as important industrial, educational, and administrative centres.

## Section 3: Population and Demographics

According to the 2011 Census of India, Tamil Nadu had a population of approximately 72.1 million people. The state has a population density of 555 persons per square kilometre, which is higher than the national average. Tamil Nadu has one of the highest urbanisation rates in India, with more than 48 percent of its population living in urban areas.

The state has a sex ratio of 996 females per 1,000 males, and a literacy rate of approximately 80.3 percent. Tamil Nadu consistently ranks among the top-performing states in India on human development indicators including health, education, and poverty reduction.

## Section 4: State Symbols

The state animal of Tamil Nadu is the Nilgiri Tahr, a mountain ungulate found in the Western Ghats. The state bird is the Emerald Dove. The state flower is the Gloriosa Lily (Kānthāl). The state tree is the Palmyra Palm (Borassus flabellifer), which has deep cultural and economic significance in Tamil culture.

## Section 5: Governance

Tamil Nadu follows the parliamentary system of governance. The state legislature is unicameral, consisting of a single house known as the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) with 234 elected seats. The state is governed by a Chief Minister who is the head of government, and a Governor who is the constitutional head appointed by the President of India. The High Court of Madras is the highest judicial authority in the state, established in 1862, making it one of the oldest High Courts in India.
