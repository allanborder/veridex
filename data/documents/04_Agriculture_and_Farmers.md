# Tamil Nadu — Agriculture and Farmers

## Section 1: Importance of Agriculture

Agriculture is a vital sector of the Tamil Nadu economy, employing approximately 45 percent of the state's workforce. Tamil Nadu's agricultural landscape is diverse, ranging from the fertile Cauvery Delta in the east to the dry zone agriculture of the central and southern regions. The state produces a wide range of crops including rice, sugarcane, bananas, groundnuts, cotton, pulses, millets, and vegetables. Tamil Nadu is one of the leading producers of bananas, turmeric, and flowers in India.

## Section 2: Major Irrigation Systems

Tamil Nadu depends significantly on irrigation for agriculture. Major irrigation sources include the Cauvery River system, the Mettur Dam (Stanley Reservoir) on the Cauvery, the Poondi Reservoir, the Chembarambakkam Lake, and a network of tanks (small reservoirs) that historically served as the backbone of agricultural water management. Tamil Nadu has the largest number of irrigation tanks in India, estimated at over 39,000 tanks. The Cauvery water-sharing dispute with Karnataka has been a long-standing inter-state issue affecting irrigation in the delta districts.

## Section 3: Kuruvai Special Package 2026

The Kuruvai cultivation season refers to the early paddy cultivation cycle that takes place from June to September in the Cauvery Delta districts of Tamil Nadu. In 2026, the Tamil Nadu government announced a Kuruvai Special Package to support farmers affected by inadequate Cauvery water releases. The Kuruvai Special Package of 2026 allocated Rs. 500 crore to compensate delta farmers and provide agricultural input assistance including subsidised seeds, fertilisers, and diesel for pump sets. The package was specifically targeted at the districts of Thanjavur, Tiruvarur, Nagapattinam, Mayiladuthurai, and Cuddalore.

## Section 4: Farmer Registry

The Tamil Nadu government initiated a comprehensive Farmer Registry to maintain an accurate digital database of all farmers in the state. This registry links farmers with their land records (patta), Aadhaar identity, and bank account details to enable direct benefit transfers and subsidy distribution. The Farmer Registry aims to eliminate ghost beneficiaries and ensure that welfare schemes reach genuine cultivators. In May 2026, the Tamil Nadu government announced a deadline for farmer registration under the Farmer Registry scheme, requiring all eligible farmers to enrol by June 30, 2026 to continue receiving agricultural subsidies and crop insurance benefits.

## Section 5: PM Fasal Bima Yojana in Tamil Nadu

Tamil Nadu participates in the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), a crop insurance scheme that provides financial support to farmers in the event of crop failure due to natural calamities, pests, or diseases. The Tamil Nadu government subsidises a portion of the premium for farmers enrolled in the scheme. The state government also operates its own Chief Minister's Comprehensive Crop Insurance Scheme (CMCCIS) for crops and weather-related events not covered under the central scheme.

## Section 6: Agri-Business and Market Linkages

The Tamil Nadu government promotes agricultural market linkages through the Tamil Nadu Agricultural Marketing Department and the Tamil Nadu Consumer Co-operative Federation (TANFED). Uzhavar Santhai (Farmers' Markets) are operated across the state to allow farmers to sell directly to consumers without middlemen, ensuring better price realisation. Horticultural exports from Tamil Nadu, particularly bananas, mangoes, and flowers, are supported through the state's Agricultural Export Policy.
