# Tamil Nadu — Local Government and Panchayats

## Section 1: Three-Tier Panchayati Raj System

Tamil Nadu operates a three-tier Panchayati Raj system as mandated by the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1992. The three tiers are: the Village Panchayat at the grassroots level, the Panchayat Union (Block Panchayat) at the intermediate level, and the District Panchayat (Mahalir Mandal) at the district level. Tamil Nadu has approximately 12,525 Village Panchayats, 385 Panchayat Unions, and 37 District Panchayats. Local body elections in Tamil Nadu are conducted every five years by the Tamil Nadu State Election Commission.

## Section 2: Urban Local Bodies

Urban governance in Tamil Nadu is managed through Urban Local Bodies (ULBs). The Chennai Metropolitan Area is administered by the Greater Chennai Corporation (GCC), which is one of the oldest municipal corporations in India, established in 1688. Other major municipal corporations in Tamil Nadu include Coimbatore, Madurai, Tiruchirappalli, Salem, Vellore, Tirunelveli, and Thoothukudi. In addition to corporations, Tamil Nadu has numerous town panchayats and special grade municipalities that govern smaller urban settlements.

## Section 3: Devolution of Powers to Local Bodies

Tamil Nadu has progressively devolved administrative and financial powers to local bodies. Under the state's decentralisation framework, Village Panchayats are responsible for local roads, street lighting, sanitation, drinking water supply at the village level, maintenance of public spaces, and implementation of centrally sponsored schemes including MGNREGS (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme) and PM Awas Yojana (Grameen). Funds, functions, and functionaries (3Fs) have been transferred to elected local bodies to strengthen grassroots democracy.

## Section 4: Women's Reservation in Local Bodies

Tamil Nadu provides 50 percent reservation for women in elected positions in all tiers of local bodies, including village panchayats, town panchayats, municipalities, and municipal corporations. This reservation policy, one of the most extensive in India, has resulted in the election of hundreds of thousands of women to local governance positions across Tamil Nadu. The state has also mandated reservation for Scheduled Caste, Scheduled Tribe, and Other Backward Class communities in proportion to their population.

## Section 5: Rural Development Schemes

The Tamil Nadu Rural Development Department implements numerous central and state-funded schemes to improve rural infrastructure and livelihoods. Key schemes include the Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY) for rural road construction, PM Awas Yojana for housing, the Swachh Bharat Mission for sanitation, and the Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation (AMRUT) for urban infrastructure. The Tamil Nadu government also operates the Kalaignar Veedu Thittam for constructing houses for the poor in rural areas.

## Section 6: Smart Village Initiative

Tamil Nadu has launched a Smart Village initiative to improve the quality of life in rural areas through technology-enabled governance. Under this initiative, village panchayats are equipped with digital service centres, high-speed internet connectivity, solar-powered street lights, and grievance redressal systems. The initiative aims to bridge the digital divide between urban and rural Tamil Nadu and improve the delivery of government services at the village level.
