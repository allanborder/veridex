# Tamil Nadu — Tourism, Culture and Heritage

## Section 1: Overview of Tourism

Tamil Nadu is one of the most visited states in India, attracting millions of domestic and international tourists each year. The state's tourism appeal lies in its ancient Dravidian temples, scenic hill stations, pristine beaches, wildlife sanctuaries, and vibrant cultural traditions. Tamil Nadu Tourism Development Corporation (TTDC) is the state agency responsible for promoting and developing tourism infrastructure. The state is particularly known for its temple architecture, classical arts, and Carnatic music tradition.

## Section 2: Major Temples and Religious Sites

Tamil Nadu is home to some of the most magnificent Hindu temples in the world, renowned for their towering gopurams (gateway towers) and intricate stone carvings. The Meenakshi Amman Temple in Madurai, one of the most visited pilgrimage sites in India, is dedicated to Goddess Meenakshi and has 14 elaborately decorated gopurams. The Brihadeeswarar Temple in Thanjavur, a UNESCO World Heritage Site built by Chola king Raja Raja Chola I in the 11th century, is a masterpiece of Tamil architecture. The Nataraja Temple in Chidambaram is dedicated to Lord Shiva in the form of Nataraja (the cosmic dancer). The Ranganathaswamy Temple in Srirangam, one of the 108 Divya Desams, is the largest functioning temple complex in India.

## Section 3: Hill Stations and Natural Attractions

Tamil Nadu has several popular hill stations located in the Western Ghats. Ooty (Udhagamandalam), known as the "Queen of Hill Stations," is located in the Nilgiris district at an altitude of approximately 2,240 metres above sea level. The Nilgiri Mountain Railway (popularly known as the Toy Train) connecting Mettupalayam to Ooty is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Kodaikanal, situated in the Palani Hills of Dindigul district, is another major hill station known for its picturesque lake and pine forests. Yercaud in the Shevaroy Hills of Salem district is a popular destination offering coffee and spice plantations.

## Section 4: Beaches and Coastal Tourism

Tamil Nadu has a coastline of approximately 1,076 kilometres, one of the longest among all Indian states, offering beautiful beaches for tourists. Marina Beach in Chennai is the world's second longest urban beach, stretching approximately 13 kilometres. Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram), located about 60 kilometres south of Chennai, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site featuring rock-cut cave temples and rathas (monolithic chariots) built by the Pallava dynasty. Rameswaram on Pamban Island is a major pilgrimage destination and connects to mainland India via the Pamban Bridge.

## Section 5: Classical Arts and Cultural Heritage

Tamil Nadu has a rich tradition of classical arts. Bharatanatyam, one of the oldest classical dance forms in India, originated in the temples of Tamil Nadu. Carnatic music, one of the two main traditions of Indian classical music, is deeply associated with Tamil Nadu and has produced legendary composers and musicians including Thyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, and Syama Sastri (the Trinity of Carnatic Music). The Chennai Music Season (Margazhi Season) held from December to January is one of the largest cultural events in the world, featuring hundreds of Carnatic music concerts and Bharatanatyam performances.

## Section 6: Wildlife Sanctuaries and National Parks

Tamil Nadu has several protected natural areas for wildlife conservation. Anamalai Tiger Reserve (formerly Indira Gandhi Wildlife Sanctuary) in Coimbatore district is home to tigers, leopards, elephants, and gaurs. Mudumalai Tiger Reserve in the Nilgiris is part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, which is the largest biosphere reserve in India. Vedanthangal Bird Sanctuary, located near Chennai, is one of the oldest and most important waterbird sanctuaries in South Asia, attracting thousands of migratory birds during winter months.
